1. Overview and goal

The Shello Skin Analyzer is a standalone, mobile-first web app for Shello Cosmetics (shellocosmetics.com — a WooCommerce shop with prices in Sri Lankan Rupees, based in Kiribathgoda, Sri Lanka). A shopper can optionally take or upload a selfie, answer a short quiz, and receive a 2–3 step routine of real, in-stock Shello face or body skin products, each with a size, an LKR price, a reason taken from that product’s own page, and a shop link.

The app does not change the shop, the CMS, or any prices. It does not diagnose or treat skin conditions. If the shopper reports painful cysts, a rash, bleeding, or a sudden change — or if a selfie is flagged the same way — the app shows a dermatologist note and no products.

Goal
Help a shopper leave with a simple, honest routine they can actually buy from the live Shello catalog, without inventing claims, prices, or sizes, and without storing their photo.

2. Users and personas

The intended user is a shopper on or around the Shello site who wants a short face or body routine in English, Sinhala, or Tamil. The quiz has no separate path for men, and no pregnancy or medical-caution question; those were noted in testing, not treated as test failures.

Three named personas were run against the live matcher on 21 November 2026 (catalog as at that test). All three received a 3-step, in-stock, in-budget face routine. Reasons were taken from the product pages.

3. User flow

The quiz is six steps (the progress bar is “step n of 6”). That is what is built today — not a four-question quiz. Climate is one step with two sub-questions. The selfie is optional and can be skipped.

  1. Landing. Brand header, language chips (EN, Sinhala, Tamil), a short explanation, the privacy note, “Start the quiz”, and the ready-made kits.
  2. Skin type. Oily, dry, combination, normal, or sensitive.
  3. Concerns and safety flags. Up to two concerns from: breakouts, oil control, dryness, hydration, dullness, uneven tone, tan, sensitivity, sun protection. On the same screen: “Is any of this true for you right now?” — painful cysts, rashes, bleeding, sudden changes. If any flag is chosen, later results show a dermatologist note and no products.
  4. Area. Face, body, or both.
  5. Air and sun. Indoor air: humid, air-conditioning, or mixed. Time outdoors: often, sometimes, or rarely. These are a light nudge in scoring, not a hard filter.
  6. Budget. Value (up to Rs 900), Everyday (up to Rs 1,950), or Generous (no cap). Band definitions come from the catalog.
  7. Optional selfie. Take a photo with the device camera, or upload one. The shopper can continue without a photo. If analysis is available, the photo is sent once to the analyze API, then discarded on Shello’s side. If analysis fails, the app retries once and then uses the quiz answers, with a short note.
  8. Results. A 2–3 step routine (Cleanse, Treat / glow, Moisturise) with size, price, a “from the product page” reason, and a shop link. A routine total. “Add all to cart” opens the Shello cart with the first product and shows follow-up links for the rest (the shop does not support adding several products in one request). “Send my routine on WhatsApp” opens a wa.me message to +94 74 116 9600, the number baked into the app.

The interface is available in English, Sinhala, and Tamil. Product names, sizes, prices, and product-page reasons stay in English, as published on the shop. The language choice is stored in the browser (localStorage key shello-lang).

4. Functional requirements (as built)

  • Load catalog.json in the browser and recommend only from its included, in-stock skin SKUs.
  • Run the quiz, kits, and matcher entirely in the browser, with no API key required for a quiz-only routine.
  • Optional selfie analysis through POST /api/analyze. GET /api/analyze reports whether analysis is configured, without exposing secrets. On 21 November 2026 the live GET returned HTTP 200 with analysis available and provider “gemini”.
  • Support in-page camera capture (getUserMedia) and file upload. Photos stay in memory for the request and are not written to disk, cache, or logs by this app.
  • Show a dermatologist-only result, with no products, when a quiz red flag is set or when selfie analysis sets refer_dermatologist.
  • Localise the chrome and quiz in English, Sinhala, and Tamil. Sinhala and Tamil still need a native-speaker proofread.
  • Open a shop add-to-cart URL for a single product, and a WhatsApp share of the routine.
  • Send page views to Vercel Web Analytics.
  • Refresh catalog prices and stock from the shared daily price list, and do a full shop rebuild on Sundays or when new product IDs appear.
  • Ship security headers, including a Content-Security-Policy, plus branded logo, favicon, apple-touch icon, Open Graph image, and theme colour #01652F.

5. Recommendation rules and safety

  • Cosmetic only. Every result carries the catalog disclaimer: recommendations are cosmetic suggestions only and are not a medical diagnosis or treatment.
  • Red flags stop the routine. Painful cysts, rashes, bleeding, sudden changes, or a selfie photo-concern flag produce a “Please see a dermatologist” message and an empty product list.
  • In-stock face and body skin products only. The matcher reads catalog.included and skips anything not in stock. Hair, deodorant, hand wash, foot cream, and bundle/combo products are excluded when the catalog is built. As of the 11:35 AM catalog: 25 products excluded (hair 15, deodorant 3, hand wash 3, bundle/combo 3, foot cream 1). 68 skin SKUs included, among them 3 hand-cream SKUs — hand cream is allowed; hand wash is not.
  • Claims only from product copy. The reason line is a sentence from the product’s short description, description, or page copy. If no sentence is found, the fallback is “As described on the product page.” The matcher does not invent ingredients, benefits, or names.
  • Two or three steps, within budget. It tries Cleanse, then Moisturise, then Treat / glow, then sorts them into that display order. A treat step is omitted if no candidate scores high enough (this is how some body + tighter-budget routines stay at two steps). Each pick must fit the remaining budget. Value prefers the smallest affordable size; Everyday prefers a daily size of about 80–200 ml when one fits; Generous prefers the largest affordable size.
  • Quiz answers win. A selfie may only fill leftover concern slots, up to two concerns in total. It does not override the shopper’s skin type or chosen concerns.
  • Climate is a nudge. Humid air slightly favours lightweight or gel products; air-conditioning slightly favours richer moisturisers. Time outdoors can boost a sunscreen — but only when the routine is not face-only, because the current catalog has no face-specific sunscreen.
  • Some safety penalties inside scoring. Scrubs are heavily penalised for sensitive skin or a sensitivity concern, and reduced for breakouts.

6. Architecture

Browser

Static HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. The quiz matcher and language pack run on the shopper’s device. catalog.json is fetched in the browser.

Vercel function

/api/analyze accepts an optional selfie in the request body, calls Gemini (or OpenAI if no Gemini key is set), and returns cosmetic appearance notes. The image is not stored.

Catalog pipeline

build_catalog.py reads the public WooCommerce Store API (read-only REST is a fallback). A daily job applies the shared shop price list, then deploys only if prices or stock changed.

Selfie models. The function prefers a configured Gemini key, and falls back to OpenAI if that key is not set. It tries Gemini models in this order: gemini-3.5-flash-lite, gemini-3.1-flash-lite, gemini-flash-lite-latest, gemini-3.8-flash, gemini-3.5-flash, gemini-flash-latest. If no Gemini key is set, it uses OpenAI gpt-4o-mini. The live GET on 21 November 2026 reported provider “gemini”; which exact model answered that day was not recorded in the GET response. This document does not include keys or environment files.

Daily refresh. The shared shop file price-list.json is refreshed at 8:00 AM Asia/Colombo. The analyzer wrapper refresh_from_shared.sh is meant to run after that (the README example is 8:15; the workstream scheduled it for around 8:30). On weekdays it copies prices and stock into the existing catalog and does not crawl the shop again or add new products. On Sunday, or when the shared list has product IDs not yet in the catalog, it runs a full refresh_and_deploy.sh rebuild so new products can be classified and given images. A 21 November 2026 dry run logged 125 shared rows, 68 included SKUs, all in stock, and no price or stock changes (shared list stamped 8:11 AM Asia/Colombo).

Analytics and security. Vercel Web Analytics is on the page. vercel.json sets Content-Security-Policy, X-Content-Type-Options, Referrer-Policy, Permissions-Policy (camera is allowed on the same origin; microphone and geolocation are not), X-Frame-Options DENY, and no-store caching on /api/*. Local development uses server.py on port 8765.

9. Privacy

The live privacy note, shown on the landing and selfie steps, is:

Live wording (unpaid Gemini terms)
Shello doesn’t save your photo. If you add a selfie, it is sent once to Google’s Gemini API so we can suggest products, then discarded on our side. We don’t write it to a database or our logs. Under Google’s unpaid Gemini API terms, Google may use submitted content (including images) to improve its products, and human reviewers may see it. This is a product suggestion, not medical advice.

As of 21 November 2026, the project was left on Google’s unpaid Gemini API terms, and this wording was still on the live site. Billing status was not re-checked at the time this document was written. If billing is turned on, this note should be updated and the app redeployed.

The analyze function sets no-store headers and is written so that the photo and the API key are not included in responses or custom logs. The Content-Security-Policy limits camera use to the same origin. Shopper photos are not used to train a Shello model today.

12. Proposed roadmap

These items are not built. They are the next steps discussed for after the current app is stable.

  1. Measure accuracy with 30–50 shoppers (or staff) rating whether the routine felt right, and mark this as the first real accuracy number. It is not measured yet.
  2. Photo quality guide and a retake check, so a dark, cropped, or blurry selfie is caught before it is sent.
  3. Let the shopper confirm the selfie read (skin-type hint and concerns) before it is merged into the quiz.
  4. Paid Gemini, then a revised privacy note that no longer describes unpaid-tier data use, and a higher selfie-throughput ceiling.
  5. Learn from clicks and sales — which recommended products are opened, added, or bought — without storing selfies.
  6. Fill catalog gaps in the shop (toner, face mask, face sunscreen, slug and size repairs) so the matcher has those steps to offer.
  7. A custom model only later, and only with consented, labelled data. That is not in progress and should not be started on unpaid-tier selfies.
